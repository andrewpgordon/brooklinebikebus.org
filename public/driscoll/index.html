Bring · and how we ride

  • A helmet. Every head, grown-ups too. No helmet, no ride.
  • Air in the tires. Squeeze them the night before.
  • Working brakes. Both. Roll the bike and squeeze.
  • Something bright. October mornings are dim — we'll have loaner vests.
  • A backpack, not a basket. Hands on the bars.
  • Stay between the leaders. Nobody passes the front or drops behind the back.
  • Two abreast, maximum.
  • We stop at every light and stop sign, then go together.
  • Call it out: "car back," "stopping."
  • No headphones.
  • Struggling? Say so — we slow down.

Questions we've been asked

My kid isn't a confident rider yet.
Four good options, all fine: ride right alongside them; put a younger one on a bike seat or a tag-along; practice a few afternoons between now and October 7; or come to this one to watch and join us on the next one. Nobody is keeping score.
Do we ride on Beacon Street?
Never. We cross it twice and both times on foot. The walking group crosses at the Summit Avenue light on the way to Griggs Park, and the whole bike bus gets off and walks across the big intersection at Washington Square together, on one light.
We don't live near either meeting point.
Doesn't matter — join wherever suits you. We stop in two places: Griggs Park, 7:15 to 7:30, and Washington Square at 7:38. Turn up at either one. You don't need to live nearby and you don't need to tell us first.
We live on the hill but can't make 7:00.
Come straight to the northeast corner of Griggs Park any time between 7:15 and 7:30, however you get there — ride, walk, or drive down. No explanation needed.
Can I drop my child off and not come?
No. Every child rides in the care of their own parent or guardian, or another adult that parent has arranged with in advance. The organizers are volunteers riding with their own kids — we are not supervising anyone else's child.
Scooter? Balance bike? Cargo bike? Tag-along?
All welcome. Scooters just need to keep up with a slow-moving group of bikes.
What if it rains?
We cancel for rain, ice, or below about 25°F. You'll hear by 6:15 AM — check before you head out.
Is this going to happen every week?
October 7 is a trial run. If enough families come, and enough grown-ups offer to help lead, we'll make it monthly.
What about riding home?
Mornings only for now. Bikes stay at the racks and kids ride home as they normally would.

Please read this before you ride

This is not a school program. The Driscoll Bike Bus is an informal ride organized by parents. It is not a program of the Michael Driscoll School, the Public Schools of Brookline, the Driscoll PTO, or the Town of Brookline, and it is not sponsored, supervised, endorsed, or insured by any of them.

The organizers are parent volunteers. We are not cycling instructors, coaches, guides, or childcare providers. We hold no qualification for any of this, and we are not supervising anyone else's child.

Every child rides in the care of their own parent or guardian — or another adult that parent has arranged with directly, in advance. That adult stays with the child for the whole ride and is solely responsible for them. Children cannot be dropped off.

Riding on public streets carries real risk of serious injury. Falls, motor vehicles, opening car doors, road surfaces, weather, mechanical failure, and the actions of other people — much of which nobody can foresee or control. The route crosses Beacon Street, a major arterial.

You are the one who decides. You know your child and you can see the weather. Judge for yourself, every time, whether they are ready and whether the day is safe for your family — and don't let the fact that a group is going substitute for that judgment. If you have any doubt, stay home or walk. Nobody will think a thing of it.

Helmets are required for every rider, adult and child, no exceptions.

The sign-up form asks you to confirm you have read this, that you are taking part voluntarily and at your own risk, that you remain responsible for supervising your own child, and that you release the organizers and other participating families from claims arising from participation, to the fullest extent Massachusetts law allows.

A parent-organized group ride, not a Driscoll School program. Each child rides with their own responsible adult.
