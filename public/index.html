How to start one →

What is a bike bus?

  • A set route and a set time. Like a bus, it has stops. You join at whichever one suits you.
  • Adults front and back. Nobody passes the leader, nobody falls behind the sweep.
  • The pace of the slowest rider. That's the whole point — a six-year-old sets the speed.
  • Every child with their own grown-up. These are parent-organized rides, not school programs and not childcare.
  • Kids arrive awake. The reliable, boring benefit, and the one teachers notice.
  • Fewer cars at the school gate — which is what makes the gate safer for everyone still walking.
  • It teaches road sense in a group, at low speed, with adults calling out what they're doing and why.
  • Once a month is a real bike bus. Weekly is lovely; monthly is sustainable. Start with once.

Starting one at your school

How many people do you need?
Four adults for about fifteen kids — one at the front, one at the back, two holding side streets. Six is better because someone always cancels. You do not need to be a cyclist, and you do not need permission from anyone to ride bicycles on a public street.
Where do you start?
Somewhere flat, off the road, with room to stand around: a park entrance, a playground gate, a quiet corner. Work backwards from the bell so you arrive fifteen minutes before it, and give yourself a fifteen-minute gathering window — that window, not the ride, is what absorbs a late family or a flat tyre.
What about hills and busy roads?
Route around them, and if you can't, walk them. Anything much over 5% downhill with young riders is worth walking, and a group of twenty is better off dismounting to cross a big intersection than trying to ride it on one light. Nobody minds walking. People mind feeling unsafe.
Who can help?
Massachusetts Safe Routes to School, a MassDOT program, will review your route, make you a shareable map, and send free safety gear for ride leaders — at no cost. They are the first call, not the last.
