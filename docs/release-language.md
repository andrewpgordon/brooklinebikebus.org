# Release language — canonical text

**This is the single source of truth.** It appears in two places: the Driscoll
ride page (`driscoll/index.html`, the `.legal` block, in a shortened form) and
the Google Form's final section (in full). If you change one, change both.

⚠️ **Not legal advice. Not reviewed by a lawyer.** See `open-questions.md`.

## What it is trying to do

1. Sever any implication of school, PSB, PTO or Town sponsorship
2. Disclaim organizer expertise — volunteers, not instructors or childcare
3. Put supervision squarely on each child's own parent, and forbid drop-offs
4. Name the actual risks specifically rather than gesturing at "risk"
5. Put the go/no-go judgment on the parent, every single ride
6. Obtain assumption of risk and a release, acknowledged with a timestamp
7. Concede up front that gross negligence can't be waived — claiming otherwise
   weakens a release rather than strengthening it
8. Say plainly what happens to photographs, and give a way out of them

## Full text (Google Form)

```
PLEASE READ THIS BEFORE YOU CHECK THE BOX.

The Driscoll Bike Bus is an informal ride organized by parents. It is NOT a program of the Michael Driscoll School, the Public Schools of Brookline, the Driscoll PTO, or the Town of Brookline. It is not sponsored, supervised, endorsed, or insured by any of them.

The organizers are parent volunteers. They are not cycling instructors, coaches, guides, ride marshals, or childcare providers. They hold no professional qualification for any of this and are not supervising anyone else's child.

EVERY CHILD RIDES IN THE CARE OF THEIR OWN PARENT OR GUARDIAN — or of another adult that parent has arranged with directly and in advance. That adult stays with the child for the entire ride and is solely responsible for them. Children may not be dropped off.

Riding a bicycle on public streets carries real risk of injury, including serious injury and death. Risks include falls, collisions with motor vehicles, other riders or pedestrians, opening car doors, road surface and weather conditions, mechanical failure, and the actions of other people — some of which no organizer can foresee or control. The route crosses Beacon Street, a major arterial road.

YOU are in the best position to judge whether your child is ready for this ride and whether conditions on the day are safe for your family. Please make that judgment yourself, every time, and do not let the existence of a group ride substitute for it. If you have any doubt at all, stay home or walk instead — nobody will think a thing of it.

Helmets are required for every rider, adult and child, with no exceptions.

PHOTOS. We take photos on the ride and may share them in the WhatsApp group, on brooklinebikebus.org, in the school newsletter, and with local news. We never post children's names with photos. If you don't want your child in photos we share, email Andrew or Nicole or tell us at the start of the ride, and we'll leave them out or blur their face. If you change your mind later, tell us and we'll remove any photo we've posted.

BY CHECKING THE BOX BELOW, I confirm that:
• I have read and understood everything above.
• I am the parent or legal guardian of the child or children I have listed.
• Our participation is entirely voluntary and at our own risk.
• I remain solely responsible for supervising my own child throughout, and for deciding whether they take part at all.
• I will not hold the organizers, other participating families, or any volunteer responsible for any injury, loss, or damage arising out of our participation, and I release them from any such claim to the fullest extent permitted by Massachusetts law.
• I understand this release does not, and cannot, cover gross negligence or willful misconduct.
• I understand photos may be taken and shared as described above, and that I can opt out at any time by telling the organizers.
```

## The photo wording, and why it's here now

**Added to this file Oct 21, 2026, after the fact.** The PHOTOS paragraph and the
last confirmation line had been live on the form for a long time and were never
written down here, which is how the ride page ended up promising the opposite.
The page said nothing went anywhere public until the family in the picture had
been asked. The form says photos may be shared unless a family opts out, and
twenty families have agreed to that version.

Andrew's call on Oct 21: **the form's version is the right one.** Opt-out, with
a way out at any time, and nothing with a child's name attached. The ride page
was rewritten the same day to say the same thing in plainer words, and D20 is
amended.

Also fixed on the form that day: the gross-negligence line had two stray copies
of "it's completely fine to stay home or walk instead." stuck to the end of it,
left over from some earlier edit. The line above is what it says now.

## MA Safe Routes to School's view (Sep 14)

Tina Hein: MA SRTS recommends a light touch on permissions, "if at all". How a
child gets to school is the family's choice, and the only liability waiver a
school has is for the school bus. If we want permission, she suggests a basic
form like the example in their pedestrian safety curriculum (Andrew has the
attachment). Undecided whether to simplify this release to match. See
`open-questions.md`.

## Standing rules

- Never describe any ride as school-sponsored, anywhere, including casually in a
  newsletter blurb.
- Never use a school's logo, crest, or branding. Driscoll's *colours* are fine —
  colours aren't trademarks in this context — but the mark is not.
- The dragon badge on the Driscoll page is our own AI-generated art and reads
  "Driscoll Bike Bus", on purpose. A version reading "Driscoll School" was
  rejected because it looked like a school seal (decision D14).
- The site footer carries a condensed version of points 1 and 3. Keep it there.
- If another school reuses this, they get it reviewed themselves. Don't let it
  spread unreviewed on the strength of "Driscoll uses it".
