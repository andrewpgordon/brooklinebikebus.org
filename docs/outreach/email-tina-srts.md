Hi Tina,

Thank you for these — the Cambridge case study and the start-up guide were
exactly what I needed. I'd like to take you up on a call. I'm copying Nicole
McClelland, who is organizing this with me.

We're planning a single pilot ride at Driscoll on **Wednesday, October 7**,
gathering at 7:15 and arriving at 7:45 for the 8:00 bell — roughly the Lincoln
Bike Bus schedule.

I've done some homework on the route and I've arrived at a specific proposal
plus a set of questions I'd rather ask you than guess at.

**Where our families live.** Most Driscoll families are up on the east shoulder
of Corey Hill — Mason Terrace, York Terrace, Lancaster Terrace, Jordan Road — at
roughly 150 to 220 feet. Those streets are contour roads and connect to each
other on flat ground. The school is at about 115 feet on the other side.

**The problem, plainly.** There is no gentle way off that shoulder. I measured
all of them: Summit Avenue is 8% average and 12% at worst, the west end of
Jordan Road is 13.4%, and the mildest option is Lancaster Terrace at a steady
6.4–7% for about a tenth of a mile. For kids who are still learning to ride,
none of those is a group descent I want to lead.

**So the plan is to keep the hill out of the bike bus.** The ride itself starts
at the northeast corner of Griggs Park, at the bottom — Griggs Terrace, Griggs
Road, then Washington Street to the school. About three quarters of a mile,
nothing over 3%, no riding on Beacon Street at all, and the one crossing of it
is done on foot with everyone dismounted. Families on the hill come down to
Griggs Park however works for them, and we run a small walking group down
Summit at 7:00 with a leader so nobody's first descent is alone.

**My questions:**

1. Is that the right call — keeping the descent out of the organized ride and
   treating it as "how you get to the start"? Or do other Massachusetts bike
   buses lead groups down grades like that, and I'm being too cautious?
2. If a walking group is the right idea, should they walk the whole way down, or
   ride Lancaster in single file with a leader setting the pace? My instinct is
   walk it, at least for the first ride.
3. Is there a line off that hill I haven't found? I've measured Summit,
   Lancaster, Jordan, Mason and the terraces, but you know this terrain better
   than a contour map does.
4. Washington Street is two lanes with parking both sides, a bus route, and
   patchy bike accommodation. Appropriate for a bike bus, and what would you
   want to see us do on it — take the lane, cork the side streets, something
   else?
5. How do other bike buses get twenty mixed-age riders across a signalised
   arterial like Beacon at Washington Square? We're planning to dismount and
   walk across on one light. Is a crossing guard something your program can help
   us request?
6. Would you ride the route with us and produce the shareable map? What do you
   need from us, and what's the lead time?
7. The free safety gear — what's in the package, and how early do I need to
   order to have it before Oct 7?
8. What waiver or permission language do other Massachusetts bike buses use with
   families? I'd rather adopt something proven than write my own.
9. Does MA Safe Routes coordinate with the school and the police department
   directly, or is that on us? If it's on us, is there anything from your
   program — a letter, a one-pager — that makes those conversations easier?

Would a call in the next week or two work? We have about four weeks and I'd
rather spend them on the right route than the first one.
